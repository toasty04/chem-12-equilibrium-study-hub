Package 4 · p. 81

#20f · Dilution with a shared ion

Mix 50.0 mL of 0.200 M CaCl₂ with 50.0 mL of 0.200 M NaCl. Find the final concentration of each ion.

Check your reasoning

Each stock is diluted twofold. [Ca²⁺] = 0.100 M and [Na⁺] = 0.100 M. Both contribute chloride, so [Cl⁻] = 2(0.100) + 0.100 = 0.300 M.

Package 5 · p. 87

#25d · Three precipitation equations

Mix aqueous barium nitrate and lithium sulfate. Write the balanced formula, complete ionic and net ionic equations, with states.

Check your reasoning

Ba(NO₃)₂(aq) + Li₂SO₄(aq) → BaSO₄(s) + 2LiNO₃(aq).
Ba²⁺(aq) + 2NO₃⁻(aq) + 2Li⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) → BaSO₄(s) + 2Li⁺(aq) + 2NO₃⁻(aq).
Cancel lithium and nitrate. Ba²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) → BaSO₄(s).

Package 6 · p. 90

#33 · Identify and separate ions

Draw a flow chart for an unknown that can contain Ag⁺, Al³⁺, both or neither. Label each reagent, precipitate and filtrate.

Check your reasoning

Unknown → add NaCl → AgCl(s) identifies Ag⁺. Filter any solid and retain the liquid. → Add NaOH dropwise → Al(OH)₃(s) identifies Al³⁺. Avoid excess hydroxide, which can redissolve Al(OH)₃. A negative test means no detectable ion at that step. Continue to the second test even if the first is negative.

Package 7 · p. 95

#46 · Ksp from solubility

A saturated sample contains 7.35 g silver acetate per litre at 25 °C. Use 166.91 g/mol to find Ksp.

Check your reasoning

s = 7.35/166.91 = 0.0440 M. Each dissolved formula unit supplies one Ag⁺ and one acetate, so Ksp = s² = 1.94 × 10⁻³.

Package 7 · p. 95

#47 · Solubility from Ksp using ICE

Find the molar solubility of Ag₂CrO₄ in pure water using Ksp = 1.1 × 10⁻¹². Set up an ICE table first.

Check your reasoning

Ag₂CrO₄(s) ⇌ 2Ag⁺(aq) + CrO₄²⁻(aq). For silver and chromate, the initial row is 0, 0. The change row is +2s, +s. The equilibrium row is 2s, s. Ksp = (2s)²s = 4s³, so s = 6.5 × 10⁻⁵ M.

Package 8 · p. 98

#59 · Will a precipitate form?

Mix 10.0 mL of 1.0 × 10⁻³ M Pb(NO₃)₂ with 40.0 mL of 1.5 × 10⁻⁴ M Na₂SO₄. Predict precipitation using Ksp(PbSO₄) = 1.8 × 10⁻⁸.

Check your reasoning

In 50.0 mL total, [Pb²⁺] = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ M and [SO₄²⁻] = 1.2 × 10⁻⁴ M before reaction. Q = 2.4 × 10⁻⁸ > Ksp. PbSO₄ precipitates.

Package 8 · p. 99

#68 · Which precipitate forms first?

Add Pb²⁺ dropwise to 0.10 M each of Cl⁻, I⁻ and SO₄²⁻. Compare the thresholds using Ksp values of 1.2 × 10⁻⁵, 8.5 × 10⁻⁹ and 1.8 × 10⁻⁸ for the respective salts.

Check your reasoning

At onset Q = Ksp. For PbCl₂ and PbI₂ divide Ksp by (0.10)² to get required [Pb²⁺] of 1.2 × 10⁻³ M and 8.5 × 10⁻⁷ M. For PbSO₄ divide by 0.10 to get 1.8 × 10⁻⁷ M. PbSO₄ reaches saturation first.

Package 8 and chloride lab · p. 101

#70 · Chloride titration

A 25.0 mL sample needs 36.8 mL of 0.100 M AgNO₃ to reach the endpoint. Find [Cl⁻]. As a lab follow-up, describe the visible endpoint.

Check your reasoning

Ag⁺ and Cl⁻ react 1:1. n(Cl⁻) = 0.0368 × 0.100 = 0.00368 mol. [Cl⁻] = 0.00368/0.0250 = 0.147 M. The white AgCl forms during titration. A persistent orange-red trace of Ag₂CrO₄ signals the endpoint.

Ksp from measured solubility

Package 7 · 1

A 250.0 mL saturated sample contains 0.0312 g of a salt MX₂ (156.0 g/mol). Find the molar solubility, both ion concentrations and Ksp.

Check your reasoning

s = 0.0312/(156.0 × 0.2500) = 8.00×10⁻⁴ M. [M²⁺] = s and [X⁻] = 1.60×10⁻³ M. Ksp = 4s³ = 2.05×10⁻⁹.

Package 7 · 2

A₂B dissolves to 0.0240 g/L. Its molar mass is 300.0 g/mol. Write the dissolution equilibrium and determine Ksp.

Check your reasoning

A₂B(s) ⇌ 2A⁺(aq) + B²⁻(aq). s = 8.00×10⁻⁵ M. Ksp = (2s)²s = 2.05×10⁻¹².

Solubility from Ksp · ICE

Package 7 · 1

Construct an ICE table for CaF₂ in pure water. Ksp = 1.5×10⁻¹⁰. Find s, [F⁻] and mass in 200.0 mL (78.07 g/mol).

Check your reasoning

Ca²⁺ | F⁻ rows: initial 0 | 0, change +s | +2s, equilibrium s | 2s. Ksp = 4s³. s = 3.3×10⁻⁴ M, [F⁻] = 6.7×10⁻⁴ M. Mass = s × 0.2000 × 78.07 = 0.0052 g.

Package 7 · 2

Ag₂CrO₄ dissolves in 0.0100 M Na₂CrO₄. Set up an ICE table and estimate s using Ksp = 1.1×10⁻¹². Check your approximation.

Check your reasoning

Ag⁺ | CrO₄²⁻ rows: initial 0 | 0.0100, change +2s | +s, equilibrium 2s | 0.0100+s. Ksp ≈ 4s²(0.0100). s ≈ 5.2×10⁻⁶ M, just 0.052% of the initial chromate.

Will a precipitate form?

Package 8 · 1

Combine 30.0 mL 4.00×10⁻⁴ M AgNO₃ with 70.0 mL 2.00×10⁻⁶ M NaCl. Compare Q with Ksp = 1.8×10⁻¹⁰.

Check your reasoning

After dilution [Ag⁺] = 1.20×10⁻⁴ M and [Cl⁻] = 1.40×10⁻⁶ M. Q = 1.68×10⁻¹⁰ < Ksp. No AgCl forms.

Package 8 · 2

Combine equal volumes of 0.0100 M Pb(NO₃)₂ and 0.00400 M MgI₂. Will PbI₂ precipitate? Ksp = 8.5×10⁻⁹.

Check your reasoning

Before precipitation [Pb²⁺] = 0.00500 M and [I⁻] = 0.00400 M (two iodides, then twofold dilution). Q = 8.00×10⁻⁸ > Ksp. PbI₂ forms.

Which precipitate forms first?

Package 8 · #68–69 · 1

Add Ag⁺ gradually to 0.020 M Cl⁻ and 0.0010 M Br⁻. Ignore dilution. Use Ksp = 1.8×10⁻¹⁰ for AgCl and 5.4×10⁻¹³ for AgBr.

Check your reasoning

[Ag⁺] thresholds: 9.0×10⁻⁹ M for AgCl and 5.4×10⁻¹⁰ M for AgBr. AgBr forms first.

Package 8 · #68–69 · 2

Add Pb²⁺ to 0.20 M I⁻ and 0.010 M sulfate. Ksp values are 8.5×10⁻⁹ and 1.8×10⁻⁸ respectively. Compare with Hebden #68.

Check your reasoning

Required [Pb²⁺] for PbI₂ = 8.5×10⁻⁹/0.20² = 2.1×10⁻⁷ M. For PbSO₄ it is 1.8×10⁻⁶ M. PbI₂ forms first at these concentrations.

Formula, complete ionic and net ionic

Packages 4–5 · 1

Mix aqueous BaCl₂ and Na₂SO₄. Write all three equations and identify spectator ions.

Check your reasoning

Formula: BaCl₂(aq) + Na₂SO₄(aq) → BaSO₄(s) + 2NaCl(aq).
Complete ionic (free ions aqueous): Ba²⁺ + 2Cl⁻ + 2Na⁺ + SO₄²⁻ → BaSO₄(s) + 2Na⁺ + 2Cl⁻.
Net: Ba²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) → BaSO₄(s). Na⁺ and Cl⁻ cancel.

Packages 4–5 · 2

Mix 20.0 mL 0.300 M AgNO₃ with 40.0 mL 0.0500 M Na₃PO₄. Write all three equations and calculate the final [Na⁺].

Check your reasoning

Formula: 3AgNO₃(aq) + Na₃PO₄(aq) → Ag₃PO₄(s) + 3NaNO₃(aq).
Complete ionic (free ions aqueous): 3Ag⁺ + 3NO₃⁻ + 3Na⁺ + PO₄³⁻ → Ag₃PO₄(s) + 3Na⁺ + 3NO₃⁻.
Net: 3Ag⁺(aq) + PO₄³⁻(aq) → Ag₃PO₄(s). [Na⁺] = 3(0.0500)(40.0/60.0) = 0.100 M.

Chloride titrations

Package 8 · lab pp. 21–22 · 1

A 20.00 mL aliquot of a fivefold diluted sample requires 12.40 mL 0.0800 M AgNO₃. Find the original chloride concentration and identify the endpoint.

Check your reasoning

Ag⁺ + Cl⁻ → AgCl(s), so n(Cl⁻) = 0.0800 × 0.01240 = 0.000992 mol. Diluted [Cl⁻] = 0.0496 M. Original = 0.248 M. Persistent orange-red Ag₂CrO₄ signals the endpoint.

Package 8 · lab pp. 21–22 · 2

Titrate 10.00 mL chloride aliquots with 0.100 M AgNO₃. Titres are 9.22, 8.14 and 8.16 mL. Use the two closest values. How would including the outlier affect your answer?

Check your reasoning

Mean = 8.15 mL. n = 8.15×10⁻⁴ mol. [Cl⁻] = 0.0815 M. Including 9.22 mL raises the mean and overestimates chloride.

Draw an identification and separation flow chart

Package 6 · #26–36 · 1

Your unknown may contain Ag⁺, Ba²⁺ and Ni²⁺. Draw a flow chart with reagents, precipitates, filtrates and a negative branch at each step.

Check your reasoning

Unknown → add NaCl → AgCl(s) identifies Ag⁺.
↓ Filter and retain liquid → add Na₂SO₄ → BaSO₄(s) identifies Ba²⁺.
↓ Filter and retain liquid → add NaOH → Ni(OH)₂(s) identifies Ni²⁺.
At each negative branch record no detectable candidate and carry the clear liquid to the next test.

Package 6 · #26–36 · 2

Design a flow chart for Cl⁻ and SO₄²⁻ using Ba(NO₃)₂ and AgNO₃. Explain why BaCl₂ would spoil the procedure.

Check your reasoning

Unknown → Ba(NO₃)₂ → solid BaSO₄ indicates sulfate → filter.
Filtrate → AgNO₃ → solid AgCl indicates chloride. Follow the same liquid path after a negative test. BaCl₂ introduces chloride and can give a false positive.

Conventions that protect your marks

  • Use the Ksp value supplied by the question or your class table. Values may differ slightly between tables and temperatures.
  • At Q = Ksp, call the solution saturated / at the precipitation threshold. Your notes call this “just starts”; Q > Ksp drives net precipitation.
  • “Insoluble” in the school table means low solubility, not exactly zero. Your Hebden classification uses less than 0.1 M.
  • For these dilute solutions use concentrations, additive volumes, and ideal dissociation as in the course. Retain guard digits and round the final answer.
  • Added solid does not change Ksp or saturated ion concentrations at fixed temperature while solid remains. Do not confuse the rate of dissolving with the equilibrium amount.
  • Some headings and handwriting are cropped in the scans. The package map follows the visible order and your package labels; unreadable details are not invented.
