SOLUBILITY CURVES · #17 SKILLS

Temperature changes the limit.

This original practice graph uses hypothetical salts A, B and C. Its vertical axis is grams of salt per 100 g of water, not grams per litre of solution.

Assume equilibrium, no evaporation, no supersaturation, and independent salts. Cooling a saturated sample from 60 to 20 °C crystallizes (50 − 30) × 150/100 = 30 g of A.

Will AgCl precipitate?

Conventions that protect your marks

  • Use the Ksp value supplied by the question or your class table. Values may differ slightly between tables and temperatures.
  • At Q = Ksp, call the solution saturated / at the precipitation threshold. Your notes call this “just starts”; Q > Ksp drives net precipitation.
  • “Insoluble” in the school table means low solubility, not exactly zero. Your Hebden classification uses less than 0.1 M.
  • For these dilute solutions use concentrations, additive volumes, and ideal dissociation as in the course. Retain guard digits and round the final answer.
  • Added solid does not change Ksp or saturated ion concentrations at fixed temperature while solid remains. Do not confuse the rate of dissolving with the equilibrium amount.
