Package 1 · pp. 74, 76

#1

Classify the dissolved particles as ions or molecules for RbBr, CHCl₃, CsNO₃, CuSO₄, S₈, CrCl₃, NaCH₃COO, ICl, HNO₃ and CH₄. Treat HNO₃ as an acid in water.

Check your reasoning

Ions: RbBr, CsNO₃, CuSO₄, CrCl₃, NaCH₃COO and HNO₃. The other four give molecular solutes.

#2

Write dissolution equations for solid ammonium sulfate, liquid ethanol, solid potassium carbonate and solid calcium chloride.

Check your reasoning

Products respectively: 2NH₄⁺ + SO₄²⁻, CH₃CH₂OH(aq), 2K⁺ + CO₃²⁻, Ca²⁺ + 2Cl⁻. Label all dissolved products (aq).

#3

Represent saturated K₃PO₄, NH₄Cl and Al(NO₃)₃ by equilibrium equations.

Check your reasoning

Place each solid on the left of ⇌. Aqueous products are 3K⁺ + PO₄³⁻, NH₄⁺ + Cl⁻, and Al³⁺ + 3NO₃⁻.

#4

Write the crystallization equation for magnesium bromide.

Check your reasoning

Mg²⁺(aq) + 2Br⁻(aq) → MgBr₂(s).

#5

Show how solid glucose dissolves.

Check your reasoning

C₆H₁₂O₆(s) → C₆H₁₂O₆(aq).

#6

Decant 100 mL of clear liquid from saturated NaCl. Is the transferred liquid saturated?

Check your reasoning

Yes, at the original temperature. Decanting leaves its concentration unchanged.

#7

Do newly added NaCl crystals sitting on the bottom establish that the water is saturated? Explain.

Check your reasoning

Allow time for equilibrium. Even an unsaturated solution takes time to dissolve solid.

Check your reasoning

#9

Check your reasoning

#11

Convert 1.2 × 10⁻⁴ M Ag₂CO₃ to grams per 100.0 mL.

#12

At 0 °C a saturated 150.0 mL sample holds 92.6 g CrO₃. Find s.

Check your reasoning

92.6 ÷ 99.99 ÷ 0.1500 = 6.17 M.

#13

How much AgClO₂ is dissolved in 50.0 mL of a saturated 0.014 M solution?

Check your reasoning

0.014 × 0.0500 × 175.32 = 0.12 g.

Package 4 · p. 81

#18

Find every ion concentration for (a) 0.25 M FeCl₃, (b) 1.5 × 10⁻³ M Al₂(SO₄)₃, (c) 12.0 g (NH₄)₂CO₃ in 2.50 L, (d) 0.41 g Ca(OH)₂ in 500 mL and (e) 2.50 g KBr in 150 mL.

Check your reasoning

Cation then anion, in M: (a) 0.25, 0.75. (b) 0.0030, 0.0045. (c) 0.0999, 0.0500. (d) 0.011, 0.022. (e) 0.140, 0.140.

#19

PbBr₂ dissolves to 0.844 g/100 mL. Write the equilibrium and find s and each ion concentration.

Check your reasoning

PbBr₂(s) ⇌ Pb²⁺(aq) + 2Br⁻(aq). 8.44/367.0 = 0.0230 M Pb²⁺ and 0.0460 M Br⁻.

#20

Calculate the final concentrations of the named ions. Volumes below are in mL and concentrations in M. (a) 20.0 of 0.35 Fe³⁺ + 25.0 water. (b) 50.0 of 0.25 Ag⁺ + 100.0 of 0.10 nitrate. (c) 15.0 of 6.5×10⁻⁵ Cu²⁺ + 40.0 of 3.2×10⁻³ chloride. (d) 55.0 of 0.185 MgCl₂ + 25.0 of 0.048 CaBr₂. (e) 95.0 of 8.65×10⁻⁴ Al(NO₃)₃ + 15.0 of 7.50×10⁻⁶ Ag₂SO₄. (f) 50.0 each of 0.200 CaCl₂ and 0.200 NaCl. (g) 25.0 of 0.360 NH₄Br + 75.0 of 0.160 (NH₄)₂SO₄. (h) 10.0 of 0.100 Ba(NO₃)₂ + 40.0 of 0.300 AgNO₃.

Check your reasoning

Add moles of any shared ion before dividing by total volume. For (f), chloride comes from both salts: (2 × 0.200 × 0.0500 + 0.200 × 0.0500)/0.1000 = 0.300 M. Calcium and sodium are each 0.100 M.

Package 5 · pp. 83–84, 87

#21

Use your solubility table to classify AgCl, NaOH, FeS, FeSO₄, Al₂(CO₃)₃, Fe(NO₃)₃, Na₃PO₄, CuI, CuCl₂ and PbBr₂.

Check your reasoning

Low solubility: AgCl, FeS, Al₂(CO₃)₃, CuI and PbBr₂. The other five are soluble in the course table.

#22

Predict the solid for each pair of 0.2 M solutions: AgNO₃/NH₄Br, SrBr₂/NaNO₃, KOH/AlCl₃, NaI/Pb(NO₃)₂, BaS/Na₂SO₄, CaS/NH₄Cl.

In order: AgBr, none, Al(OH)₃, PbI₂, BaSO₄, none.

#23

Use solubility to explain the two opening examples involving BaSO₄ and AgBr.

Check your reasoning

Both salts have low solubility. A large amount of solid can coexist with a small concentration of dissolved metal ions.

#24

Choose two soluble salts to prepare each precipitate: PbCl₂, AgBr, Cr₂S₃ and SrSO₄.

Check your reasoning

Use the metal nitrate with NaCl, NaBr, Na₂S and Na₂SO₄, respectively, in the course solubility-table model.

#25

For equal volumes of 0.2 M solutions, predict both products and identify each solid. For every precipitation write formula, complete ionic and net ionic equations. Pairs: (a) MgS/Sr(OH)₂, (b) CuBr₂/Pb(NO₃)₂, (c) FeBr₃/SrI₂, (d) Ba(NO₃)₂/Li₂SO₄, (e) K₃PO₄/CuCl₂, (f) (NH₄)₂SO₃/Al₂(SO₄)₃, (g) AgNO₃/Na₃PO₄, (h) ZnSO₄/FeCl₂, (i) CoSO₄/Li₂CO₃, (j) Fe(NO₃)₃/MgS, (k) BeSO₄/(NH₄)₂CO₃, (l) MgSO₄/Sr(OH)₂.

Check your reasoning

Use the course double-replacement model. In (l), check both cross-products: Mg(OH)₂ and SrSO₄ can both precipitate. See the written bank for fully worked equation sets.

Package 6 · pp. 90–91

#26

Why select a soluble reagent when removing a cation by precipitation?

Check your reasoning

The reagent must supply dissolved counterions.

#27

Use the class table to identify possible single cations if separate aliquots (a) precipitate with sulfate and hydroxide or (b) precipitate only with sulfate.

Check your reasoning

Intersect the relevant rows of your solubility table. For example Pb²⁺ fits (a) and Ba²⁺ fits (b).

#28

Can a precipitation test distinguish Ag⁺ from Pb²⁺? Use the class solubility table to justify the test.

Check your reasoning

Use a reagent whose anion forms a low-solubility salt with only one candidate. For example NaF gives PbF₂ while AgF is soluble.

#29

Order soluble reagents to separate Al³⁺ and Ag⁺.

Check your reasoning

NaCl removes AgCl. Filter, then use a controlled addition of NaOH to precipitate Al(OH)₃.

#30

Give a selective reagent sequence for Sr²⁺, Ca²⁺ and Ag⁺.

Check your reasoning

Use the class table at each step. Remove AgCl first, filter, then distinguish the remaining ions using the hydroxide entries before recovering the final ion.

#31

Separate Mg²⁺, Pb²⁺ and Zn²⁺. Name the soluble reagents in order.

Check your reasoning

NaCl → PbCl₂. Filter. Na₂S → ZnS in the course table. Filter. NaOH → Mg(OH)₂.

#32

Separate Fe³⁺, Ca²⁺, Ag⁺ and Be²⁺ using the class table. Give the reagent and solid at each step.

Check your reasoning

Start with NaCl for Ag⁺. After each filtration, reconsider only the ions remaining in the filtrate.

#33

Turn #29 into a flow chart that handles both ions, one ion or neither.

Check your reasoning

At each selective step, a precipitate is a positive result. Continue with filtrate, or with the clear sample if the test was negative.

#34

Turn #30 into a procedure that works for any subset of its three ions.

Check your reasoning

Record each reagent and solid, include a no-solid branch, then test the liquid for the remaining candidates.

#35

Identify I⁻, SO₄²⁻ and OH⁻ using AgNO₃, Ca(NO₃)₂ and Mg(NO₃)₂. Name this type of analysis, decide the first reagent and finish the sequence.

Check your reasoning

Qualitative analysis. Mg²⁺ isolates hydroxide first. Filter, then Ca²⁺ tests for sulfate. Finally Ag⁺ tests for iodide.

#36

Design a selective test sequence for S²⁻, OH⁻, Cl⁻ and CO₃²⁻ using silver, barium, copper(II) and strontium nitrates. Follow the class solubility table.

Check your reasoning

Ba²⁺ removes carbonate. After filtration use a selective hydroxide test from the course table, then Cu²⁺ for sulfide and Ag⁺ for chloride. Verify each proposed step against all remaining ions.

#37

A 25.0 mL Ba²⁺ sample gives dry BaSO₄. The filter weighs 1.21 g before collection and 3.75 g afterwards. Find precipitate moles and original [Ba²⁺].

Check your reasoning

2.54 g ÷ 233.39 g/mol = 0.0109 mol. The 1:1 ratio gives 0.435 M Ba²⁺.

Package 7 · pp. 91–92, 95

#40

Write dissolution equilibria and Ksp expressions for BaSO₄, MgF₂, Ag₂S and Cu(IO₃)₂.

Check your reasoning

The respective products are [Ba²⁺][SO₄²⁻], [Mg²⁺][F⁻]², [Ag⁺]²[S²⁻] and [Cu²⁺][IO₃⁻]².

#41

Rank AgCl, AgI and AgBr in pure water using their Ksp values.

Check your reasoning

Most to least soluble: AgCl, AgBr, AgI. All three have s = √Ksp.

#42

FeCO₃ is at equilibrium with 5.0×10⁻⁶ M Fe²⁺ and 6.0×10⁻⁶ M carbonate. Find Ksp.

Check your reasoning

Multiply the measured values: 3.0×10⁻¹¹.

#43

Find [Zn²⁺] above ZnS in pure water using the class Ksp table.

Check your reasoning

In the course model [Zn²⁺] = s = √Ksp.

#44

Find the PbSO₄ mass that dissolves in 5.0 L.

Check your reasoning

With Ksp = 1.8×10⁻⁸, mass = √Ksp × 5.0 × 303.26 = 0.20 g.

#45

Find dissolved BaCrO₄ mass in 10.0 L of saturated solution.

Check your reasoning

Mass = √Ksp × 10.0 L × 253.32 g/mol.

#46

7.35 g silver acetate saturates 1.00 L at 25 °C. Find Ksp.

Check your reasoning

s = 7.35/166.91 = 0.0440 M. Ksp = s² = 1.94×10⁻³.

#47

Calculate s for Ag₂CrO₄.

Check your reasoning

Ksp = (2s)²s. With Ksp = 1.1×10⁻¹², s = 6.5×10⁻⁵ M.

#48

Convert the Ksp of CaSO₄ into its solubility in g/L.

Check your reasoning

Multiply √Ksp by 136.14 g/mol.

#49

Convert the Ksp of Fe(OH)₂ into g/L.

Check your reasoning

Molar solubility = ∛(Ksp/4). Multiply by 89.86 g/mol.

#50

For Ag₂S, equilibrium [S²⁻] = 1.6×10⁻¹⁶ M and [Ag⁺] = 2.6×10⁻¹⁷ M. Find Ksp.

Check your reasoning

Square the measured silver concentration and multiply by sulfide: 1.1×10⁻⁴⁹.

#51

0.00014 g ZnCO₃ just saturates 100.0 mL. Determine Ksp.

Check your reasoning

s = 0.00014/(125.39 × 0.1000). Ksp = s² ≈ 1.2×10⁻¹⁰.

#52

For saturated Zn(OH)₂ with Ksp = 4.1×10⁻¹⁷, find [OH⁻].

Check your reasoning

[OH⁻] = 2∛(Ksp/4) = 4.3×10⁻⁶ M.

#53

Find [Cd²⁺] above Cd(OH)₂ when Ksp = 5.3×10⁻¹⁵.

Check your reasoning

[Cd²⁺] = ∛(Ksp/4) = 1.1×10⁻⁵ M.

#54

Find the mass of dissolved Pb²⁺ in 5.0 L of saturated lead(II) iodate.

Check your reasoning

Use 5.0 × ∛(Ksp/4) × 207.2. The requested mass is lead alone.

#55

In two saturated Mn(OH)₂ mixtures, trial 1 has [Mn²⁺] = 2.1×10⁻⁵ M and [OH⁻] = 1.0×10⁻⁴ M. Trial 2 has [Mn²⁺] = 7.8×10⁻⁴ M. Find its [OH⁻].

Check your reasoning

Both trials have Ksp = 2.1×10⁻¹³. Take √(Ksp/[Mn²⁺]) = 1.6×10⁻⁵ M.

Package 8 · pp. 98–99

#56

Mix 1.0 L each of 3.0×10⁻¹⁰ M Fe²⁺ and 1.2×10⁻⁸ M S²⁻. Predict precipitation using Ksp(FeS).

Check your reasoning

Both concentrations halve. Compare Q = 9.0×10⁻¹⁹ with your table value.

#57

Determine the sulfide threshold for CuS in 0.20 M CuCl₂.

Check your reasoning

[S²⁻] = Ksp(CuS)/0.20.

#58

Find the fluoride threshold for CaF₂ in 3.0×10⁻³ M Ca(NO₃)₂. Ksp = 1.5×10⁻¹⁰.

Check your reasoning

[F⁻] = √(Ksp/[Ca²⁺]) = 2.2×10⁻⁴ M.

#59

Mix 10.0 mL 1.0×10⁻³ M Pb(NO₃)₂ with 40.0 mL 1.5×10⁻⁴ M Na₂SO₄. Will PbSO₄ form?

Check your reasoning

Q = (2.0×10⁻⁴)(1.2×10⁻⁴) = 2.4×10⁻⁸. This exceeds 1.8×10⁻⁸, so yes.

#60

At onset, 20.0 mL 3.00×10⁻³ M Ni²⁺ has been mixed with 60.0 mL 2.52×10⁻⁴ M CO₃²⁻. Find Ksp.

Check your reasoning

Ksp = (0.00300 × 20/80)(0.000252 × 60/80) = 1.42×10⁻⁷.

#61

Mix 25.0 mL 1.0×10⁻⁴ M Zn(NO₃)₂ with 45.0 mL 2.4×10⁻⁵ M Ca(OH)₂. Does Zn(OH)₂ form if Ksp = 4.1×10⁻¹⁷?

Check your reasoning

Q = (1.0×10⁻⁴ × 25/70)(2 × 2.4×10⁻⁵ × 45/70)² ≈ 3.4×10⁻¹⁴. Yes.

#62

Ca(OH)₂ reaches onset when 100.0 mL 0.040 M CaCl₂ meets 150.0 mL 0.029 M NaOH. Calculate Ksp.

Check your reasoning

Ksp = (0.040 × 100/250)(0.029 × 150/250)² = 4.8×10⁻⁶.

#63

Add 0.050 mL of 5.0 M Pb²⁺ to 100.00 mL of 3.0×10⁻⁵ M iodide. Will PbI₂ form?

Check your reasoning

Q ≈ 2.2×10⁻¹², below 8.5×10⁻⁹. No precipitate.

#64

Mix 20.0 mL 5.0×10⁻⁵ M Ca²⁺ and 35.0 mL 2.5×10⁻⁴ M oxalate. Reduce the volume to 25.0 mL. Predict precipitation.

Check your reasoning

Use final volume for both concentrations. Q = (4.0×10⁻⁵)(3.5×10⁻⁴) = 1.4×10⁻⁸. Compare with Ksp for calcium oxalate.

#65

What carbonate concentration reaches the detection threshold when [Ag⁺] = 0.050 M?

Check your reasoning

[CO₃²⁻] = Ksp(Ag₂CO₃)/(0.050)².

#66

Dilute 5.0 mL 1.0×10⁻⁹ M Na₂S to 1.0 L. Mix 10.0 mL of this with 90.0 mL 2.0×10⁻¹⁶ M Pb²⁺. Find Ksp at onset.

Check your reasoning

Sulfide is diluted twice to 5.0×10⁻¹³ M. [Pb²⁺] = 1.8×10⁻¹⁶ M. Ksp = 9.0×10⁻²⁹.

#67

Mn(IO₃)₂ has s = 4.78×10⁻³ M. Find the manganese threshold in 0.0200 M KIO₃.

Check your reasoning

Ksp = 4s³. Required [Mn²⁺] = 4s³/(0.0200)² = 1.09×10⁻³ M.

#68

Add lead(II) ions gradually to a solution with 0.10 M each of chloride, iodide and sulfate. Rank the onset thresholds.

Check your reasoning

Required [Pb²⁺]: sulfate 1.8×10⁻⁷ M, iodide 8.5×10⁻⁷ M, chloride 1.2×10⁻³ M. PbSO₄ forms first.

#69

Add 0.02 M AgNO₃ gradually to a solution with 1.0 M each of carbonate, iodate and chromate. Which solid appears first?

Check your reasoning

For carbonate and chromate use √(Ksp/[anion]); for iodate use Ksp/[anion]. AgIO₃ reaches its silver threshold first with the course constants.

Package 8 · p. 101

#70

A 25.0 mL NaCl aliquot consumes 36.8 mL of 0.100 M AgNO₃ with chromate indicator. Determine [Cl⁻].

Check your reasoning

The 1:1 reaction gives 0.00368 mol chloride. Divide by 0.0250 L to get 0.147 M.

#71

What volume of 0.0988 M chloride reacts with the silver in 25.0 mL of 0.0750 M AgNO₃?

Check your reasoning

n(Ag⁺) = 0.0250 × 0.0750 mol. V(Cl⁻) = n/0.0988 = 0.0190 L or 19.0 mL.

#72

Dissolve 3.25 g KCl to make 500.0 mL. A 25.00 mL aliquot consumes 9.48 mL silver nitrate. Find its molarity.

Check your reasoning

n(KCl) = 3.25/74.55 mol. The aliquot contains 25.00/500.0 of that amount. [AgNO₃] = (3.25/74.55)(25.00/500.0)/0.00948 = 0.230 M.

Package 9 · p. 108

#81

Sr(OH)₂ has solubility near 0.5 M at 25 °C. Give two changes that increase the amount that dissolves and two that decrease it.

Check your reasoning

Increase dissolution by consuming OH⁻ with acid or removing Sr²⁺ as SrSO₄ with sulfate. Suppress it with NaOH or Sr(NO₃)₂. Each change alters an ion in Sr(OH)₂(s) ⇌ Sr²⁺ + 2OH⁻.

#82

Explain why adding solid BaCO₃ to its saturated solution fails to raise the equilibrium solubility.

Check your reasoning

Pure solid is absent from the Ksp expression. Extra solid changes neither temperature nor equilibrium ion concentrations.

#83

How could you dissolve a calcium carbonate coating on a metal plate? Explain using the equilibrium.

Check your reasoning

Acid consumes carbonate: CaCO₃(s) + 2H⁺(aq) → Ca²⁺(aq) + CO₂(g) + H₂O(l). Removing carbonate favours further dissolution.

#84

Give two ways each to increase and decrease PbCl₂ dissolution.

Check your reasoning

Increase it by removing Cl⁻ as AgCl using AgNO₃ or Pb²⁺ as PbSO₄ using Na₂SO₄. Decrease it by adding NaCl or Pb(NO₃)₂. These predictions use the class equilibrium model.

#85

Compare SrCl₂ dissolution in four 1 M solutions: NaNO₃, Na₂SO₄, Sr(NO₃)₂ and MgCl₂. Which gives the greatest and least solubility in the course model?

Check your reasoning

Greatest: Na₂SO₄ removes Sr²⁺ as SrSO₄. Least: MgCl₂ supplies 2 M common chloride. The dissolution expression contains [Cl⁻]².

#86

Rank NaBr solubility, greatest first, in 1 M NaCl, 1 M AgNO₃, 1 M KNO₃, 1 M Na₂SO₄ and 2 M AgNO₃. Explain the effects of both Ag⁺ and Na⁺.

Check your reasoning

2 M AgNO₃ > 1 M AgNO₃ > 1 M KNO₃ > 1 M NaCl > 1 M Na₂SO₄. Silver removes bromide as AgBr, KNO₃ contributes no common ion, and Na₂SO₄ supplies twice as much common sodium as NaCl.

Ksp from measured solubility

Package 7 · 1

A 250.0 mL saturated sample contains 0.0312 g of a salt MX₂ (156.0 g/mol). Find the molar solubility, both ion concentrations and Ksp.

Check your reasoning

s = 0.0312/(156.0 × 0.2500) = 8.00×10⁻⁴ M. [M²⁺] = s and [X⁻] = 1.60×10⁻³ M. Ksp = 4s³ = 2.05×10⁻⁹.

Package 7 · 2

A₂B dissolves to 0.0240 g/L. Its molar mass is 300.0 g/mol. Write the dissolution equilibrium and determine Ksp.

Check your reasoning

A₂B(s) ⇌ 2A⁺(aq) + B²⁻(aq). s = 8.00×10⁻⁵ M. Ksp = (2s)²s = 2.05×10⁻¹².

Solubility from Ksp · ICE

Package 7 · 1

Construct an ICE table for CaF₂ in pure water. Ksp = 1.5×10⁻¹⁰. Find s, [F⁻] and mass in 200.0 mL (78.07 g/mol).

Check your reasoning

Ca²⁺ | F⁻ rows: initial 0 | 0, change +s | +2s, equilibrium s | 2s. Ksp = 4s³. s = 3.3×10⁻⁴ M, [F⁻] = 6.7×10⁻⁴ M. Mass = s × 0.2000 × 78.07 = 0.0052 g.

Package 7 · 2

Ag₂CrO₄ dissolves in 0.0100 M Na₂CrO₄. Set up an ICE table and estimate s using Ksp = 1.1×10⁻¹². Check your approximation.

Check your reasoning

Formula, complete ionic and net ionic

Packages 4–5 · 1

Mix aqueous BaCl₂ and Na₂SO₄. Write all three equations and identify spectator ions.

Check your reasoning

Formula: BaCl₂(aq) + Na₂SO₄(aq) → BaSO₄(s) + 2NaCl(aq).
Complete ionic (free ions aqueous): Ba²⁺ + 2Cl⁻ + 2Na⁺ + SO₄²⁻ → BaSO₄(s) + 2Na⁺ + 2Cl⁻.
Net: Ba²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) → BaSO₄(s). Na⁺ and Cl⁻ cancel.

Packages 4–5 · 2

Mix 20.0 mL 0.300 M AgNO₃ with 40.0 mL 0.0500 M Na₃PO₄. Write all three equations and calculate the final [Na⁺].

Check your reasoning

Formula: 3AgNO₃(aq) + Na₃PO₄(aq) → Ag₃PO₄(s) + 3NaNO₃(aq).
Complete ionic (free ions aqueous): 3Ag⁺ + 3NO₃⁻ + 3Na⁺ + PO₄³⁻ → Ag₃PO₄(s) + 3Na⁺ + 3NO₃⁻.
Net: 3Ag⁺(aq) + PO₄³⁻(aq) → Ag₃PO₄(s). [Na⁺] = 3(0.0500)(40.0/60.0) = 0.100 M.

Chloride titrations

Package 8 · lab pp. 21–22 · 1

A 20.00 mL aliquot of a fivefold diluted sample requires 12.40 mL 0.0800 M AgNO₃. Find the original chloride concentration and identify the endpoint.

Check your reasoning

Ag⁺ + Cl⁻ → AgCl(s), so n(Cl⁻) = 0.0800 × 0.01240 = 0.000992 mol. Diluted [Cl⁻] = 0.0496 M. Original = 0.248 M. Persistent orange-red Ag₂CrO₄ signals the endpoint.

Package 8 · lab pp. 21–22 · 2

Titrate 10.00 mL chloride aliquots with 0.100 M AgNO₃. Titres are 9.22, 8.14 and 8.16 mL. Use the two closest values. How would including the outlier affect your answer?

Check your reasoning

Mean = 8.15 mL. n = 8.15×10⁻⁴ mol. [Cl⁻] = 0.0815 M. Including 9.22 mL raises the mean and overestimates chloride.

Conventions that protect your marks

  • Use the Ksp value supplied by the question or your class table. Values may differ slightly between tables and temperatures.
  • At Q = Ksp, call the solution saturated / at the precipitation threshold. Your notes call this “just starts”; Q > Ksp drives net precipitation.
  • “Insoluble” in the school table means low solubility, not exactly zero. Your Hebden classification uses less than 0.1 M.
  • For these dilute solutions use concentrations, additive volumes, and ideal dissociation as in the course. Retain guard digits and round the final answer.
  • Added solid does not change Ksp or saturated ion concentrations at fixed temperature while solid remains. Do not confuse the rate of dissolving with the equilibrium amount.
